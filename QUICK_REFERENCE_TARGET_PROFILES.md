# Target Resource Profile - Quick Reference

## 🎯 Two Types of Profiles

### 1️⃣ Introduction/Concept Videos
**Unit Types:** `topic`, `prerequisite`

**Format:** Concise (2-3 sentences)

**Example:**
```
A video explaining Newton's Second Law clearly, defining force, mass, 
and acceleration. The video should cover the relationship F=ma with 
real-world examples and demonstrate how to apply it to simple problems.
```

**Use Case:** Learning new concepts, understanding theory

---

### 2️⃣ Problem Walkthrough Videos  
**Unit Type:** `walkthrough`

**Format:** Full problem statement + solution approach

**Example:**
```
A video solving this problem: A 2000 kg car accelerates from rest to 
25 m/s in 8 seconds on a level road. The coefficient of friction is 0.15. 
Calculate the force applied by the engine and the distance traveled during 
acceleration. The video should show step-by-step calculations using 
Newton's Second Law and kinematic equations, explaining the relationship 
between force, friction, and acceleration, and demonstrating how to solve 
for both the applied force and distance.
```

**Use Case:** Solving specific problems, step-by-step guidance

---

## 📋 What's Included

### Introduction Videos Include:
- ✅ Concept name
- ✅ Key terms to define
- ✅ Main relationships/formulas
- ✅ Example applications
- ✅ Learning objectives

### Walkthrough Videos Include:
- ✅ **COMPLETE problem statement**
- ✅ All given values with units
- ✅ All unknowns to find
- ✅ All conditions and constraints
- ✅ Equations to use
- ✅ Concepts to explain
- ✅ Solution approach

## ⚡ Quick Tips

### DO ✅
- Copy the entire problem_statement field for walkthroughs
- Include ALL numerical values with units
- Keep introduction profiles concise
- Focus on what the video should teach

### DON'T ❌
- Abbreviate problem statements
- Skip numerical values
- Make walkthrough profiles generic
- Mix the two formats

## 💡 Examples by Subject

### Physics - Introduction
```
A video explaining projectile motion clearly, defining trajectory, 
range, and maximum height. The video should cover the kinematic 
equations for 2D motion with real-world examples like basketball 
shots or cannon fire.
```

### Physics - Walkthrough
```
A video solving this problem: A ball is thrown at 30 m/s at an angle 
of 45° from a cliff 50 m high. Calculate the time of flight, maximum 
height above the cliff, and horizontal range. The video should show 
step-by-step calculations using kinematic equations for projectile 
motion, explaining the independence of horizontal and vertical motion, 
and demonstrating how to find each quantity.
```

### Circuits - Introduction
```
A video explaining RC circuits clearly, defining time constant, 
charging, and discharging behavior. The video should cover the 
exponential response equations with examples of capacitor charging 
through a resistor.
```

### Circuits - Walkthrough
```
A video solving this problem: A series RC circuit has R = 1kΩ and 
C = 10μF. A 12V step voltage is applied at t=0. Find the voltage 
across the capacitor as a function of time and calculate the time 
constant. The video should show step-by-step calculations using 
RC circuit equations, explaining capacitor charging behavior and 
time constant significance, and demonstrating how to derive the 
voltage function.
```

### Thermodynamics - Introduction
```
A video explaining the First Law of Thermodynamics clearly, defining 
internal energy, heat transfer, and work. The video should cover the 
energy balance equation with examples of closed systems undergoing 
thermodynamic processes.
```

### Thermodynamics - Walkthrough
```
A video solving this problem: A piston-cylinder device contains 2 kg 
of steam at 200°C and 300 kPa. Heat is added until the temperature 
reaches 400°C at constant pressure. Calculate the heat transfer and 
work done using steam tables. The video should show step-by-step 
calculations using the First Law and steam property tables, explaining 
constant pressure processes and property evaluation, and demonstrating 
how to find both heat transfer and boundary work.
```
